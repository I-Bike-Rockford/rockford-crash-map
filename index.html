    High-injury network and crash rates

    Two questions come up whenever people look at a crash map. Where is the danger concentrated? And how dangerous is one kind of street compared with another? This page answers both and shows the numbers.

    What we found

    • A small share of streets holds a large share of the harm. About 2.5% of the city's road miles (20.5 miles) hold 41% of the fatal and serious-injury crashes. Add a second tier and 4.7% of road miles (37.7 miles) hold 60%.
    • Arterials are far more dangerous per mile. A mile of principal arterial had about 25 times as many fatal and serious-injury crashes as a mile of local street.
    • State-owned roads stand out. They are 7% of the city's road miles and had 29% of these crashes.
    • The network is mostly arterials. 91% of its length is principal or minor arterial. 35% is state-owned, 12% county-owned and 53% city-owned.
    • It captures most walking and biking harm. The network holds 59 of the 80 fatal and serious-injury crashes involving a person walking or biking (74%), and 61 of the 92 fatal crashes (66%).
    • Walking and biking crashes cluster even more tightly. 14 half-mile stretches, 0.8% of road miles, hold 44% of those walking and biking crashes on their own.

    The high-injury network on the map

    Turn on Show high-injury network under Map options. It appears as an orange or gold line, the same weight everywhere, over the gray road lines:

    • Tier 1 (orange): half-mile stretches of street with 3 or more fatal or serious-injury crashes in the five years.
    • Tier 2 (gold): half-mile stretches with 2 more such crashes, after Tier 1 is set aside.
    • Walking and biking view (orange): half-mile stretches with 2 or more fatal or serious-injury crashes involving a person walking or biking.

    The network is fixed to 2021–2025. It does not change when you use the year filter.

    Crash rates per street mile

    A rate is the number of crashes divided by the miles of street, per year. It lets us compare a short, busy street with a long, quiet one. Here, a rate is fatal and serious-injury crashes per centerline mile per year, 2021–2025.

    Miles are centerline miles from IDOT's road layers, for streets inside the city limits: 807 in all. A divided road counts once for each direction, and ramps are included. Of the 388 fatal and serious-injury crashes inside the city in these years, 370 could be matched to one of those streets and are counted here. The other 18 were too far from a mapped road.

    What to keep in mind

    • Small numbers. Many streets have only a few serious crashes in five years. Differences of one crash are noise. Treat the tiers as groups, not as a precise ranking.
    • No traffic counts. These are rates per mile of street, not per mile driven, walked or biked. A busy street will show more crashes simply because more people use it. We did not have traffic volumes for every street.
    • Crashes, not causes. The network says where serious harm has happened. It does not say why, and it does not say who was at fault.
    • Police-reported crashes only. Crashes that were not reported, and near misses, are not counted. Walking and biking crashes are more likely to be under-reported.
    • Matching is imperfect. A crash on a side street near an arterial can be matched to the arterial. Each crash is matched to the nearest mapped road within 100 feet, and where it is within 25 feet of two roads, such as at an intersection, to the higher-class road. We used the same rule for every crash.
    • The half-mile window is a choice. A different window or a different cutoff would give a somewhat different network. A ten-year version of the network, using a higher bar of 4 crashes in a half mile, overlaps 84% of the five-year Tier 1 length, so the main corridors do not depend on the five years we chose.
    • Not a priority list. A safety program should also consider who lives nearby, how many people walk and bike there, and what can be fixed. This page does not.

    About the data: methodology and limitations

    Who makes this map

    This map is an initiative of I Bike Rockford, a volunteer group. It is not a product of the City of Rockford, the Illinois Department of Transportation (IDOT), or any employer or firm. It is offered in good faith as a public resource.

    What the map shows

    Crashes on Rockford streets from 2006 through 2025 that were reported to police and entered in the State of Illinois crash database. The map highlights crashes in which someone was killed or seriously injured, with a focus on people walking and biking.

    Beyond mere data points, each dot represents a person: a cherished friend, a beloved family member. The map shows attributes gathered from public records.

    Where the data comes from

    • Crash records: Illinois Department of Transportation (IDOT) statewide crash data, 2006–2025. Rockford crashes were selected from IDOT's files. Records for 2016–2025 come from IDOT's Safety Data Mart extracts; records for 2006–2015 come from IDOT's earlier GIS crash extracts. This map contains IDOT crash data only, apart from the person-level facts about fatal crashes described next.
    • Person-level facts about deaths (role and age band): the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS), 2006–2024. IDOT's public crash files describe the crash, not the people in it, so for fatal crashes we matched IDOT records to FARS by date, time and location. Of 266 IDOT fatal crashes in 2006–2024, 258 (97%) matched confidently, and in every one of those both sources record the same number of people killed. The other eight had no confident match and show no person-level facts. FARS does not contain names. It covers deaths only (not injuries), and 2025 is not yet published, so 2025 fatal crashes show no person-level facts.
    • City limits: City of Rockford city limits, from the City's public GIS service (retrieved October 2026).
    • Data as of: October 2026. IDOT's most recent extract (2025) is dated July 22, 2026. IDOT revises past years after the fact, so counts can change slightly. The map ends at 2025. Data for 2026 will be added when IDOT publishes it.

    How we prepared it

    IDOT changed its data layout several times between 2006 and 2025. We combined all years into one consistent format: the same field names, the same category labels, and one location system. We repaired formatting errors in IDOT's files, removed 13 duplicate records, and tested every location against the city limits. Every field is described on the Data Dictionary page.

    What the terms mean

    Crash severity follows the standard KABCO scale and is assigned by the police officer at the scene, based on the most severe injury in the crash:

    Code Meaning
    K Fatal: a person died
    A Serious injury: an incapacitating injury (for example, a person who could not walk away)
    B Minor injury: visible, non-incapacitating injury
    C Possible injury: complaint of pain, no visible injury
    O No injury (property damage only)

    "Pedestrian" and "bicycle" crashes are crashes IDOT classifies as a pedestrian or pedalcyclist collision. "Serious injury crash" means a crash with at least one A-level injury, not the number of people seriously injured.

    What this map cannot tell you

    These numbers are a minimum, not a complete count.

    • Only police-reported crashes are included. Many crashes involving people walking and biking are never reported: when no one calls the police, when the person is hurt but leaves, or when no motor vehicle stops. Crashes reported later by phone or online are more likely to be missing or incomplete. The data to estimate how many are missed does not exist.
    • Near misses and falls are invisible. A person who swerves to avoid a car, hits a pothole, or falls off a bike and is hurt, without colliding with anything or filing a report, does not appear. Neither do the many close calls that never become a crash. A street can feel dangerous long before it shows up in this data.
    • Severity is an officer's judgment at the scene, not a medical finding. Injuries that prove more serious later may not be updated, and a crash can be reclassified after a death that occurs days later.
    • Locations are approximate. IDOT often places crashes at an intersection's center or along a street segment. Many crashes can share one point; where that happens the map groups them and shows a count. A dot is not an exact spot on the pavement.
    • People walking and biking are identified by the type of first collision recorded in the report. Some crashes that injured a pedestrian or cyclist may be coded differently and are not counted as such here.
    • No fault is assigned. The map does not show who caused a crash.
    • Recent years can change. The most recent data is the least complete.
    • Counts are not rates. More crashes on a street can simply mean more people use it. This map shows counts, not risk per mile traveled or per person.
    • Trends need care. The number of crashes and the way they are classified shift over 20 years because of changing reporting practices, forms, and events such as the 2020 pandemic. Dips and spikes (for example, an unusually high number of deaths in 2013 and 2021) should be checked against other sources before drawing conclusions.
    • City limits: about 3% of the crashes IDOT labels "Rockford" (2,251 of about 82,000) plot outside the current city limits, most of them within 150 feet of the line, on streets that run along the boundary. They are shown on the map as faded points and are left out of the headline totals unless you choose to include them. About 1,300 crashes since 2016 plot inside the city limits but are labeled by IDOT as unincorporated or as another town; they are not shown.
    • Street names come from IDOT for 2025 only. For earlier years the map shows the location but not a street name.
    • FARS differences: FARS counts a death if it happens within 30 days of the crash and applies its own coding rules, so its counts can differ from IDOT's. Roles (for example, person walking) come from FARS where matched and can differ from the collision type IDOT records.

    Using this data

    You are welcome to use the map and the downloadable data for advocacy, research, planning and reporting. Please credit "I Bike Rockford, using Illinois Department of Transportation crash data" and note the data-as-of date. Crash records are from IDOT. This map is not an official IDOT or City product, and its figures may differ from official statistics.

    Language we use

    We say "crash," not "accident," because crashes have causes that can be addressed. We say "person killed" or "person seriously injured."

    Data

    Everything on the map comes from crash records of the Illinois Department of Transportation (IDOT), 2006–2025, plus person-level facts about fatal crashes from the federal FARS database. Download the crash file to explore it yourself.

    Download crashes, 2006–2025 (CSV)

    Please credit: “I Bike Rockford, using Illinois Department of Transportation crash data.” The file has one row per crash (81,979 rows), including 39 crashes with no usable location and crashes that plot just outside the city limits (see the IN_CITY and XY_QA columns). It contains no names. Road class and road owner come from IDOT’s statewide functional class and jurisdiction map layers, matched to each crash by location (see the dictionary below); they describe the road, not who was at fault. The address search uses property addresses from Winnebago County parcel data. Nothing about owners is used or published. Read About the data before drawing conclusions; counts are minimums, not rates.

    Data dictionary

    Field Format Description
    CRASH_ID Text (20) Unique crash identifier. 2016–2025: IDOT Illinois Crash Number (ICN), 12 digits, e.g. 202501010371. 2006–2015: legacy report ID, format YY-NNNNNNN, e.g. 06-1268553. Unique within every layer. Use it as the key for joining other data.
    SRC Text (20) Source batch the record came from: 'Legacy 2006-2015' (older IDOT GIS extracts) or 'IDOT SDM extract' (Safety Data Mart statewide extracts, 2016–2025).
    YEAR Integer (4) Crash year, four digits (e.g. 2019).
    CRASH_DATE Text (10) Crash date as ISO text YYYY-MM-DD (e.g. 2025-12-31). Built from year, month and day so it reads the same in every year.
    MONTH Integer (2) Month of crash, 1–12.
    DAY Integer (2) Day of month, 1–31.
    HOUR Integer (2) Hour of crash on a 24-hour clock, 0–23 (0 = midnight hour). Minutes are not carried; IDOT dropped crash minute from the 2025 extract.
    DOW Text (9) Day of week, full name (Monday … Sunday).
    SEVERITY Text (1) Crash severity on the KABCO scale, by the most severe injury in the crash: K = fatal, A = suspected serious injury, B = suspected minor injury, C = possible injury, O = no injury (property damage only), U = unknown (record had no severity).
    SEV_LABEL Text (34) Plain-language label for SEVERITY: Fatal; A - Serious injury; B - Minor injury; C - Possible injury; No injury (property damage only); Unknown.
    N_KILLED Integer (3) Number of people killed in the crash.
    N_INJURED Integer (3) Total number of people injured in the crash, all severities A+B+C (does not include fatalities).
    N_A_INJ Integer (3) Number of people with A (serious) injuries. 2016–2025 only; blank for 2006–2015 because the legacy extracts carry only a total.
    N_B_INJ Integer (3) Number of people with B (minor) injuries. 2016–2025 only.
    N_C_INJ Integer (3) Number of people with C (possible) injuries. 2016–2025 only.
    N_VEH Integer (2) Number of motor vehicles involved.
    COLL_TYPE Text (30) Type of first collision / first harmful event as coded by IDOT, harmonized across eras: Angle, Animal, Fixed Object, Front to Front, Front to Rear, Other Non-Collision, Other Object, Overturned, Parked Motor Vehicle, Pedalcyclist, Pedestrian, Rear to Front, Rear to Rear, Rear to Side, Sideswipe Opposite Direction, Sideswipe Same Direction, Train, Turning, Unknown.
    MODE Text (10) Vulnerable-road-user flag derived from COLL_TYPE: 'Pedestrian' (COLL_TYPE = Pedestrian), 'Bicycle' (COLL_TYPE = Pedalcyclist), else 'Other'. LIMITATION: based on first collision type only. A crash where a pedestrian or cyclist was hurt but the first event was something else is coded 'Other'.
    INTERSEC Text (1) Intersection-related crash: Y or N (as coded by the reporting officer).
    HIT_RUN Text (1) Hit-and-run: Y or N. 2016–2025 only; blank for 2006–2015.
    CAUSE1 Text (80) Primary contributing circumstance coded by the reporting officer (e.g. 'Failing to Yield Right of Way'). 2016–2025 only. Officer-coded and preliminary; NOT a finding of fault.
    CAUSE2 Text (80) Secondary contributing circumstance, same coding as CAUSE1. 2016–2025 only. '(N/A)' means none recorded.
    WEATHER Text (24) Weather condition: Clear, Cloudy/Overcast, Rain, Snow, Blowing Snow, Sleet/Hail, Freezing Rain/Drizzle, Fog/Smoke/Haze, Severe Cross Wind, Other, Unknown.
    LIGHTING Text (24) Lighting condition: Daylight, Dawn, Dusk, Darkness, Darkness - Lighted Road (written 'Darkness, Lighted Road'), Unknown.
    SURF_COND Text (20) Road surface condition: Dry, Wet, Snow or Slush, Ice, Sand, Mud, Dirt, Other, Unknown.
    RD_DEFECT Text (24) Road defect: No Defects, Worn Surface, Rut, Holes, Shoulders, Debris On Roadway, Construction Zone, Maintenance Zone, Utility Work Zone, Work Zone - Unknown, Other, Unknown. (Work-zone values appear only in the older extracts; newer extracts record work zones separately, see WORKZONE.)
    TRAF_CNTRL Text (28) Traffic control device at the crash location: No Controls, Traffic Signal, Stop Sign/Flasher, Yield, Lane Use Marking, School Zone, Pedestrian Crossing Sign, Flashing Control Signal, RR Crossing Gate/Sign, Other RR Crossing, Police/Flagman, No Passing, Delineators, Other Warning Sign, Other Regulatory Sign, Other, Unknown.
    TC_FUNC Text (26) Whether the traffic control was working: Functioning Properly, Functioning Improperly, Not Functioning, Worn Reflective Material, Missing, Other, Unknown. 2016–2025 only; blank when there was no control or for 2006–2015.
    WORKZONE Text (1) Crash in a work zone: Y or N. Blank = could not be determined (about 680 records in 2019 where IDOT's source file is misaligned).
    ROUTE Text (12) IDOT route number of the road (0 = none/local street). Numbers only, not street names.
    STREET Text (60) Street name of the crash location. Only available in the 2025 extract; blank for all other years.
    AT_INTERS Text (80) Nearest intersection as 'Street A/Street B'. 2025 only; blank otherwise.
    LAT Decimal (12,7) Latitude, WGS84 decimal degrees, computed from the point geometry.
    LON Decimal (12,7) Longitude, WGS84 decimal degrees, computed from the point geometry.
    XY_QA Text (32) Location quality flag. 'OK' = normal. 'FROM_IDOT_TS_XY' = geometry was invalid so IDOT's own coordinate columns were used. 'CHECK_OUTSIDE_EXPECTED_EXTENT' = IDOT assigns it to Rockford but the point is outside the expected city area; verify before mapping. 'NO_LOCATION' = no usable coordinates (such crashes appear in the CSV but are not mapped).
    IN_CITY Text (1) Y = the crash point falls inside the City of Rockford limits polygon (City of Rockford GIS, Parcel Viewer 'City Limits' layer, downloaded October 2026); N = IDOT labels it a Rockford crash but the point is outside that polygon; blank = no usable location. All IDOT-labeled Rockford crashes are kept; use this field to filter.
    ROAD_CLASS Text Functional class of the road the crash was matched to, from the Illinois Department of Transportation (IDOT) functional class layer: Interstate or freeway, Principal arterial, Minor arterial, Collector or Local street. Matched by location to the nearest mapped road within 100 feet; where a crash is within 25 feet of more than one road (such as at an intersection) the higher-class road is used. Blank if no road is within 100 feet.
    ROAD_OWNER Text Who has jurisdiction over that road, from the IDOT jurisdiction layer: State (IDOT), County, City (any city or village), or Township or other (includes the Tollway). Matched the same way as ROAD_CLASS. Blank if no road is within 100 feet.
    FARS_MATCH Text (9) For fatal crashes in 2006–2024 matched to the federal FARS database: 'confirmed' (same date, within one hour and 0.25 mile) or 'probable' (slightly looser). Blank = not matched, not fatal, or 2025 (FARS has not published 2025).
    FARS_ROLES_AGE Text People killed, from FARS: role (driver, passenger, person walking, person bicycling) and five- or ten-year age band, separated by semicolons when more than one person died. No names.
    FARS_SEX Text Sex of each person killed, from FARS, in the same order as FARS_ROLES_AGE.
    DRUNK_DRIVER_INVOLVED Text (1) Y if FARS records at least one driver with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.08 or higher in the crash; N otherwise. Matched fatal crashes only.

    Notes

    • Coordinates: LAT and LON are WGS84 decimal degrees.
    • Blank values: a blank means the item was not collected for that year (for example, cause codes before 2016) or was not recorded; it does not mean "no".
    • Category labels were standardized across years because IDOT changed its wording over time. The original wording is preserved where it carries a different meaning.
    • Source: Illinois Department of Transportation crash data, 2006–2025.
